H_1 = An MPC fed with forecasts of EVs, PV, building and battery comes close to the oracle (the same MPC with perfect forecasts) for several operator profiles (cost, peak and grid, users), and the gap to the oracle shrinks as forecast quality improves.
H_2 = A conditional generative diffusion model that samples the joint session tuple (departure, duration and energy, given arrival) gives more accurate and better calibrated probabilistic forecasts than GMM, LightGBM and quantile regression, and this improves the MPC outcome.
H_3 = A joint mixture over arrival, duration and energy, with the number of components chosen by BIC and a daily session count from a time series model (SARIMA), generates more realistic EV scenarios than the factorized GMM with fixed components used by Zhang et al.
H_4 = At the size of an ACN site (tens of chargers) and with per-bus grid constraints, a monolithic MPC with one variable per EV delivers the same energy at lower cost and peak than a hierarchical aggregator-distributor MPC, with acceptable solve time; the hierarchy only pays off above some number of chargers or when the solve time exceeds the control step.
H_5 = User-declared input (estimated departure and requested energy) only helps the MPC when its error is small; above some error a history-trained predictor does better, and the best option combines both by correcting the input with a model, to be tested with real user errors, unlike Zhang et al. and Engel et al. (synthetic Gaussian noise) and McClone et al. (perfect input).
